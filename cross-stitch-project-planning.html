Plan your next cross-stitch project with Pip

Starting a new design should feel exciting. These simple steps help you gather the supplies you need, make a realistic plan, and leave room to enjoy the stitching.

1. Read the pattern key

Find the thread brand, color numbers and any stated skein amounts. Check the stitch width and height, recommended fabric count, number of strands, and special instructions such as backstitch or blending. Stitch counts are not the same as skein quantities.

Use Find Floss to review listed colors and learn about alternate brands if necessary.

2. Check what you already own

Compare the project colors against your thread collection. Make a note of partial skeins and uncertain counts. A color you own is not automatically enough to complete a large design.

3. Calculate the fabric to cut

Use your pattern's stitch dimensions, fabric count and desired finishing margin. Aida and evenweave can work differently when stitching over one or over two fabric threads, so choose the correct setting before cutting. Pip's fabric calculator will show the finished design and suggested cutting dimensions.

4. Make one shopping list

In Shopping, choose “Take a combined shopping list” and select your projects. Floss Fox adds their requirements together and subtracts your confirmed collection once. Uncertain amounts stay under “Check first” so you can review them before buying.

Use “Found” while walking the shop. It is a checklist mark: it does not mark a project purchase or add thread to your collection. Record purchases and received thread separately when appropriate.

You can also share a list of thread codes and quantities. Shared links open for reading; importing creates a separate project only when the recipient chooses. A link can be forwarded and is not encrypted. Project notes, photos and journal entries are not included.

5. Leave yourself an easy return path

Save where you stopped, even if it is just “finish the lower-left border.” Open the project's Stitching journal to record a date, stitches, minutes and notes. Any thread usage you record is a log, not an automatic deduction from your collection. Update your project percentage manually when it helps.

Sessions and progress stay in this browser and are included in the full backup. Progress photos and short notes make a long project easier to return to. Pip can help you find the saved project again without insisting on daily streaks or deadlines.

Finishing means more than a percentage: the stitched piece might become a gift, card, ornament, framed picture or keepsake. Your choice is the ending that matters.
